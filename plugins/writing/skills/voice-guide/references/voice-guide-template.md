# Voice guide template

Copy this into `VOICE.md` and replace every `<…>` with what the corpus shows. Keep the
section numbers: the other writing skills refer to them (§1 for the numbers, §9 for the
anti-patterns, §10 for the ghostwriter contract). Leave a section out only if the corpus
really has nothing for it, and say so.

Instructions in *italics* are for whoever fills in the template. Delete them.

## 9. Anti-patterns

The tells that a draft was generated rather than written. None of these appear in my writing.

- Stock openers and fillers ("In today's fast-paced world", "It's important to note that",
  "Let's dive in!", "In conclusion, we've explored")
- Tricolons and built-up contrasts ("not just X, but Y", "it's not about A, it's about B")
- Headings that only label: "Introduction", "Understanding X", "Best practices",
  "Key takeaways"
- Uniform paragraph length
- A summary-bullet recap at the end
- Hedge stacking ("it's worth considering that it may potentially")
- Enthusiasm without a cost named
- Em-dash overuse
- Explaining the reader's feelings to them ("You might be thinking...")

## 10. The ghostwriter contract

The remit is to take a seed (a quick note, a voice memo, a handful of bullets) and produce a
full piece in my voice. Expansion is the job, but it has a hard boundary, and that boundary
is not length.

### You may invent freely

- **Structure**: sections, order, headings, the opening and the close.
- **Argument development**: carrying a position I stated to its consequences, and the
  reasoning that connects two things I put side by side.
- **Analogies and everyday illustrations.**
- **Explicitly hypothetical scenarios**, framed as such.
- **Generic technical illustrations**: snippets showing a documented feature of a public
  spec or tool.

### You may never invent

- **Statistics, percentages or measured claims.**
- **Quotes attributed to real people, citations or links.**
- **Anything I did**: engagements, customers, employers, conversations, what a tool did when
  I ran it.
- **Positions I haven't taken.** You can develop a stated opinion; you can't decide what I
  think about something my note is silent on.
- **Verified behaviour of a specific tool or version.**

### What research may supply

A statistic, a quote, a citation, a link or what a specification says may come from a source
you opened yourself and list with the draft (the `fact-check` skill). Research never supplies
what I did or what I think.

### When the seed lacks a fact you need

First see whether it can be looked up. If it can't, ask me, specifically, in a form I can
answer in one sentence from a phone. Where a gap is small enough to write around, write around
it and mark the spot in the hand-over notes rather than stalling the draft.
